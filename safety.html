2 · Honey mushroom field lesson

Study Armillaria spp. and ringless Desarmillaria spp. as separate hypotheses. No single feature confirms a specimen.

  • Cap often honey-tan with variable scales; appearance changes with species and maturity.
  • Gill tissue often pale. Attachment and discoloration must be observed.
  • Some Armillaria have a membranous ring. Desarmillaria lacks a persistent ring.
  • Often clusters at roots, stumps, or decaying wood; buried roots can make them appear terrestrial.
  • Typical spore deposit is pale/white, but white spores are not proof of identity.
  • Rhizomorphs may be present but are not a stand-alone species confirmation.

Look-alikes to investigate

Galerina marginata
DEADLY · AMATOXINS

Wood-associated small brown mushroom; rusty-brown spore deposit. A spore print is informative, never sufficient alone. Mixed clusters and immature specimens require particular care.

Omphalotus illudens
POISONOUS

Orange to yellow-orange true gills, clustered at wood or buried roots; can cause severe gastrointestinal poisoning.

Hypholoma spp.
SOME POISONOUS

Wood-clustering species, often with darkening gills and purplish-brown spore deposits; species-level differences matter.

Pholiota spp.
IDENTIFICATION REQUIRED

Wood-inhabiting, often scaly or sticky-capped, generally brown-spored; several resemble immature honey mushrooms.

3 · Release decision belongs elsewhere

Identification review and commercial release authorization must be distinct, audited actions.

  • Default lot state: quarantine / HOLD
  • Uncertain, mixed or conflicting determinations: HOLD
  • Independent qualified determination: documented separately
  • Safety, sourcing and compliance sign-off: separate authorized process
  • Recall link: every processed product must trace back to source lot

No sale or consumption decision is made by this page.

4 · Emergency response

If anyone may have consumed a misidentified wild mushroom, contact Poison Control in the U.S. at 1-800-222-1222. For severe symptoms, call 911. Keep remaining specimens, photos, meal information, and collection details; do not wait for symptoms.
