## Researcher-reported observations

Researcher described seeing increasing numbers of spores, using the microscope to examine apparent cell-wall boundaries, and taking annotated photographs of selected objects. These remarks are field notes, not independent evidence for spore identity or wall thickness.

## Image-based observations

Numerous discrete, high-contrast microscopic bodies occur in the imaged preparations. Their projected outlines range from near-circular to oval, irregular and locally flattened/reniform. Bright-to-dark peripheral rims produce strong boundary contrast. The rims are consistent with optical interfaces, but they do not independently resolve the ultrastructure or thickness of the spore wall. Internal optical contrast is variable; a definitive germ pore, hilar appendage, septation, ornamentation or other taxonomically diagnostic feature cannot be established from these screenshots. Several fields show clustered or overlapping objects associated with brownish granular aggregates and finer threadlike material of undetermined identity.

The apparent hyaline-to-pale character of isolated objects depends on transmitted illumination and display settings; pigmentation should be assessed on a controlled mount. The onscreen linear marks and micrometer values in images 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 12 are instrument/software annotations. They should not be treated as validated measurements until camera calibration, objective magnification, measurement axis, scale traceability and individual-object selection are documented. Apparent variable sizes may reflect specimen differences, focal plane, orientation and overlapping objects.

## Interpretation and limitations

The researcher labels the preparation as a spore print. The imaged bodies are therefore **provisionally described as spore-like structures**, not confirmed mature spores from an identified taxon. A precise wall-thickness value, spore-size distribution or diagnostic spore shape classification cannot be stated without calibrated repeat measurements and unambiguous isolated objects. No taxonomic assignment, toxicity assessment, or identification is made on the basis of these images alone.

## Recommended measurement protocol

Record microscope make/model, objective numerical aperture and magnification, imaging modality, mount medium, stage micrometer calibration on the same optical settings, and software calibration details. Measure at least 20–30 independently selected unobscured objects when available (preferably more for a robust distribution). Separate long and short axes and report sample size, range, mean, standard deviation, Q ratio and confidence limits when appropriate. Do not infer wall thickness from a brightfield edge halo. Preserve all originals and calculate cryptographic checksums.
